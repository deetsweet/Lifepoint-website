Staying awake is one of the big themes of the New Testament. The writers seemed to expect that one of the greatest dangers for followers of Jesus wouldn't be open rebellion, but slowly drifting off. Our new series, **Staying Awake**, works through 1 and 2 Thessalonians, 2 Peter and Jude: urgent letters to ordinary people.

<blockquote class="verse"><p>So then, let us not be like others, who are asleep, but let us be awake and sober.</p><p>1 Thessalonians 5:6</p></blockquote>

Paul wrote this to comfortable people in a wealthy city, even while they were facing opposition for their faith. Comfortable Australian Christianity is a **fatigue zone**, a place where it's very hard to stay awake. This message is for followers of Jesus. If you're exploring faith, you're very welcome to listen in. The good news for everyone is that God knows you, loves you, and wants to transform your life.

## Seven signs of a sleepy Christian

1. **Your hopes and dreams are mostly about yourself and your comfort.** We were called to "serve the living and true God" (1:9) and to live lives "worthy of God, who calls you into his kingdom" (2:12).
2. **Worship feels stale and gratitude feels optional.** "Rejoice always, pray continually, give thanks in all circumstances" (5:16–18). Stale worship usually means our picture of God has shrunk.
3. **You're constantly busy and barely have time to pray.** "Pray continually" (5:17). If we can get through the week without prayer, we may have stopped relying on God.
4. **Your faith is a private part of your life.** "The Lord's message rang out from you" (1:8). Not a loud or judgemental faith, but a life that makes people ask questions.
5. **You often feel like God is vaguely disappointed in you.** "Brothers and sisters loved by God… he has chosen you" (1:4). God loves you as you are, not as you should be.
6. **Your walk with Jesus looks the same as it did five years ago.** "We urge you… to do so more and more" (4:10). There is always more of God.
7. **You think more about pleasing people than pleasing God.** "We are not trying to please people but God, who tests our hearts" (2:4).

## The only thing that wakes us up

We can't wake ourselves up by trying harder. Like a Driver Reviver on a long highway, the only real answer is to pull over and rest in the love of God.

<blockquote class="verse"><p>He died for us so that, whether we are awake or asleep, we may live together with him.</p><p>1 Thessalonians 5:10</p></blockquote>

**This week:** set a daily alarm to wake up to God, and try kneeling to pray (Psalm 95:6), as a posture of need and of receiving. And remember: "The one who calls you is faithful, and he will do it" (5:24).

## Going deeper

Bible study questions on the key passages from this message.

### 1 Thessalonians 5:1–11 · Awake and sober

1. Paul contrasts "night" and "day", "darkness" and "light". What do these pictures say about who a follower of Jesus is, not just what they do?
2. Verse 8 describes faith, love and hope as armour. Why do you think Paul links staying awake with putting something on?
3. Verse 11 ends with "encourage one another". How does staying awake depend on other people?

### 1 Thessalonians 1:2–10 · A faith that rings out

1. What do verses 3 and 9 say the Thessalonians' faith actually looked like day to day?
2. They "turned to God from idols" (v9). What are the idols that compete for our hopes and dreams today?
3. How did their faith become "known everywhere" (v8) without them being loud or judgemental?

### 1 Thessalonians 4:1–12 · More and more

1. Paul twice urges them to grow "more and more" (vv1, 10). Why might people who are already doing well need to hear this?
2. Verse 8 connects how we live with the Holy Spirit God gives us. How does that change the way we think about spiritual growth?

### 1 Thessalonians 5:16–24 · God is faithful

1. Which of the commands in verses 16–22 comes most naturally to you, and which is hardest?
2. Verse 24 says "The one who calls you is faithful, and he will do it." How does this promise change the way you read the commands before it?
