When the explorers Burke and Wills were stranded at Cooper Creek, they survived on bread made from nardoo seed. It filled them up, but prepared the wrong way it stripped their bodies of nutrients. Days before he died, Wills wrote that it gave him "the greatest satisfaction" as far as appetite went. They were full, but starving.

That describes much of Australian life today. We have more food, freedom, money and leisure than almost any culture in history, and yet people are starving for meaning, hope, peace and joy. As C.S. Lewis put it, if we find a desire that nothing in this world can satisfy, perhaps we were made for another world.

## The sign isn't the point

The day after Jesus fed 5,000 people, the crowd chased him across the lake wanting more bread.

<blockquote class="verse"><p>You are looking for me, not because you saw the signs I performed but because you ate the loaves and had your fill. Do not work for food that spoils, but for food that endures to eternal life.</p><p>John 6:26–27</p></blockquote>

The miracle was a sign, and a sign is never the destination. It points to something better. When we settle for the good things of this world, we're stopping at the sign instead of going where it points.

## I am the bread of life

Jesus pointed to himself: "I am the living bread that came down from heaven. Whoever eats this bread will live forever" (6:51). Then he went further: "Unless you eat the flesh of the Son of Man and drink his blood, you have no life in you" (6:53). To his Jewish listeners this was deeply offensive, and many of his followers turned away (6:66).

There's an echo of an Old Testament story. When three of King David's mighty men risked their lives to bring him water from the well at Bethlehem, David wouldn't drink it. To him it was "the blood of men who went at the risk of their lives" (2 Samuel 23:17). Jesus says the opposite: the only way to life is to receive his sacrifice. He didn't just risk his life for us. He gave it.

## Jesus doesn't do halves

**Jesus doesn't want to add to your life. He wants to become your life.** God blesses all kinds of people in all kinds of ways, but Jesus didn't come to hand out bread rolls. Everyone has a "ruling passion", the love that organises everything else, and Jesus won't share that place with anything. As Charles Spurgeon said, "If Christ is not all to you, he is nothing to you." Paul counted everything as loss "because of the surpassing worth of knowing Christ Jesus my Lord" (Philippians 3:8).

## Where else would we go?

The one survivor of the Burke and Wills party, John King, lived because he turned to the people of the creek who had been close by all along and asked for help. Jesus is close too.

<blockquote class="verse"><p>Lord, to whom shall we go? You have the words of eternal life.</p><p>John 6:68</p></blockquote>

The gifts of God are good, but the gifts of God are not God. What is the "nardoo" you're filling up on while still starving? Jesus doesn't just want to help you. He wants to rescue you, and to be everything to you.

## Going deeper

Bible study questions on the key passages from this message.

### John 6:25–35 · Food that endures

1. What were the crowd really looking for when they followed Jesus across the lake (vv24–26)?
2. What do you think Jesus means by "food that spoils" and "food that endures" (v27)?
3. In verse 35 Jesus promises those who come to him "will never go hungry". What kind of hunger is he talking about?

### John 6:47–58 · Eat my flesh, drink my blood

1. Why would these words have been so shocking to a Jewish audience? (See Leviticus 17:11.)
2. How do these verses point ahead to Jesus' death on the cross, and to communion?

### 2 Samuel 23:13–17 · The water from Bethlehem

1. Why did David pour the water out instead of drinking it?
2. How does this story help us understand what Jesus offers in John 6?

### John 6:60–69 · To whom shall we go?

1. Why did "many of his disciples" turn back (v66)? What teachings of Jesus do people find hard to accept today?
2. What does Peter's answer in verses 68–69 show about what he had come to believe?
